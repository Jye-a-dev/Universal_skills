# References

Finished reference screens by Damir Sokolovsky ([@heydamir](https://x.com/heydamir)), viewable online at [heydamir.design/unslop](https://heydamir.design/unslop). Review their patterns before the Visuals pass. Take their craft principles, never their exact subject, palette or layout, so every product receives its own distinct screen.

| Case Study | What it shows | Why it works |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Invoicing Hero** | An invoicing hero with a paper plane drawn in digits and currency signs, small notes pinned to it, and the product rising from below | The visual is made of the product's own material, money, so it is the moment and the signature at once. Text sits on calm sky, the chrome is quiet, and the product view invites a scroll |
| **Finance Dashboard** | A finance dashboard with two rendered payment cards and a revenue chart drawn as soft colored curves over a mirrored grey comparison | Quiet graphite chrome, and all the color lives in two moments, a real looking object and a chart where every color means a series |
| **Incident Monitoring Hero** | An incident monitoring hero drawn as one blue engraving of a lighthouse on a rocky coast, with a serif headline in the same ink | A print style working because the whole page is in it. One ink, one paper tone, and the headline sits in the calm sky the drawing leaves for it |
| **Soft 3D Meadow Footer** | A footer as a soft 3D meadow with a few matte crystal shapes and a huge wordmark standing behind the hills | Friendly stylized 3D with a restrained palette, type that becomes part of the scene, and quiet links on the open sky |
| **Step-by-Step Morphing** | A vibecoded dashboard unslopping itself step by step in a short progression | Everything morphs in place in a wave, red redlines measure the slop and turn white when fixed, copy rolls letter by letter, and the frame is square and cropped close on the sidebar and first cards |

What they share: one bright element per screen, quiet chrome around it, a visual that can be explained in one sentence, and headlines placed on space that was left empty for them.
